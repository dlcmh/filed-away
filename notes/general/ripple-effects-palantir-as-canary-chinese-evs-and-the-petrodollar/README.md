### 🎯 The "Wrong Bet" on Closed-Source AI

The post argues that the US bet on proprietary AI and captive allies is failing. The events of the last week support this:

1. **Closed-Source Value is Crumbling**: As the post notes, you're paying a massive premium for AI models that are, at best, marginally better than open-source alternatives. This is a tough sell for any CFO, especially when open models like China's DeepSeek deliver comparable performance at a fraction of the cost [[5]](https://m.economictimes.com/opinion/et-commentary/open-and-shut-source-case-theres-method-in-palantir-ceo-alex-karps-mad-outburst-against-big-ai-overselling-corporates/amp_articleshow/132167808.cms). The global AI economy could save an estimated **\$25 billion annually** by shifting from closed to open models [[5]](https://m.economictimes.com/opinion/et-commentary/open-and-shut-source-case-theres-method-in-palantir-ceo-alex-karps-mad-outburst-against-big-ai-overselling-corporates/amp_articleshow/132167808.cms). This is the mathematical reality undermining the closed-source AI business model.
2. **The "Kill Switch" Fear is Real**: The Palantir-Nvidia deal for the US government has sent a clear signal to the rest of the world: Washington is building its own self-sufficient AI machine [[14]](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/technology/tech-news/palantir-and-nvidia-are-building-ai-for-the-us-that-countries-across-the-world-are-in-fear-of-americas-kill-switch/amp_articleshow/132112534.cms). This triggers a primal fear of the "kill switch"—a dependency that can be turned off at another nation's whim. Countries are rationally responding to that fear by investing in their own sovereign capabilities.
3. **US Diplomacy is Backfiring**: Under Secretary of State Jacob Helberg's recent statements have inadvertently validated these fears. By dismissing AI sovereignty as "marching into the past" and a path to "synchronized mediocrity," he is essentially telling other nations: "Our technology is your only option, and your sovereignty is our problem" [[4]](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/usa-news/india-crucial-tech-ally-as-us-cautions-nations-against-expensive-ai-sovereignty-drives/)[[8]](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/india-crucial-tech-ally-as-us-cautions-nations-against-expensive-ai-sovereignty-drives/article71165068.ece). As the X post suggests, if you're a European or Asian leader, this is a massive wake-up call to build your own stack, not a compelling reason to buy American.

### 💎 The Bottom Line

The X post is correct. Palantir is the canary, but the mine they're warning about is the one where **their own old business model** is collapsing. They are pivoting to a new narrative because the old one—"trust us with your data"—is no longer working. Governments are showing them the door because they are a symbol of the very dependency they claim to solve.

The sequence is telling: first, the Nvidia partnership to sell open models to the US government (June 29) [[3]](https://investor.wedbush.com/wedbush/article/bizwire-2026-6-29-palantir-launches-engine-for-deploying-nvidia-nemotron-open-models-in-sovereign-environments)[[11]](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/palantir-ceo-reveals-us-government-clients-shift-to-nvidia-s-open-source-ai-models). Then, the "AI sovereignty manifesto" warning everyone else about the dangers of dependency (June 30) [[5]](https://m.economictimes.com/opinion/et-commentary/open-and-shut-source-case-theres-method-in-palantir-ceo-alex-karps-mad-outburst-against-big-ai-overselling-corporates/amp_articleshow/132167808.cms). Finally, the CEO's outburst condemning closed-source AI (July 1) [[5]](https://m.economictimes.com/opinion/et-commentary/open-and-shut-source-case-theres-method-in-palantir-ceo-alex-karps-mad-outburst-against-big-ai-overselling-corporates/amp_articleshow/132167808.cms). It's a perfectly choreographed, self-serving pivot.

But as the post notes, being self-serving doesn't make them wrong. They have correctly identified the tectonic shift: **the era of unquestioned American technological hegemony, where closed-source AI was the standard and allies were captive customers, is ending.** The question now is who can build the new, sovereign infrastructure. And as we've been discussing, the race is very much on. 🙀
