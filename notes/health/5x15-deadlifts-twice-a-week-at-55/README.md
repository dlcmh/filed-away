## DeepSeek

For two 55-year-olds, doing 5 sets of 15 deadlift reps twice a week is a **bold plan, but one that carries a very high risk of injury** and might be counterproductive to your goals[[2]](https://powerliftingtechnique.com/high-rep-deadlift/#Strength-Plateau)[[5]](https://archive.t-nation.com/training/high-rep-deadlifts-are-dead/#content).

The main concerns are the **high volume and frequency** (75 total reps/week), the **technical demands of high-rep deadlifts**, and the **recovery needs at 55**[[2]](https://powerliftingtechnique.com/high-rep-deadlift/#Strength-Plateau)[[5]](https://archive.t-nation.com/training/high-rep-deadlifts-are-dead/#content)[[10]](https://k-hub.felda.net.my/neuaxis/Record/upm-92600/Description#tabnav).

- **High Reps Risk Form Breakdown**: Technique often fails after 3-5 reps; by rep 15, fatigue can cause a rounded back, straining the spine[[2]](https://powerliftingtechnique.com/high-rep-deadlift/#Strength-Plateau)[[5]](https://archive.t-nation.com/training/high-rep-deadlifts-are-dead/#content). For longevity, prioritize **near-perfect form every rep**[[2]](https://powerliftingtechnique.com/high-rep-deadlift/#Strength-Plateau).
- **Recovery is King**: At 55, recovery takes longer. Heavy deadlifts stress the nervous system; experts often recommend deadlifting **just once a week** for masters athletes[[6]](https://www.strongfirst.com/community/threads/deadlift-advice-sought.24801/#post-493404)[[9]](https://www.mensfitness.com/news/68-year-old-powerlifter-workout-routine)[[12]](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/strength-training/433/1221).
- **Goal vs. Reality**: 15 reps build endurance, not peak strength[[8]](https://barbend.com/for-strength-gains-avoid-high-rep-training/?utm_source=newsletter.barbend.com&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=3-push-pull-legs-routines-you-ve-gotta-try)[[11]](https://torokhtiy.com/blogs/guides/high-rep-deadlifts). If you're aiming for bone density and muscle mass (vital for healthy aging), 8-12 reps with proper form is a safer "sweet spot"[[4]](https://spel-int-ha.upm.edu.my/artikel/move_more_live_longer_why_malaysias_elderly_must_embrace_combined_training-83981)[[7]](https://www.urbansportsrehabcentre.com/latestnews/nid/186351/)[[10]](https://k-hub.felda.net.my/neuaxis/Record/upm-92600/Description#tabnav).

### 💪 A Safer, More Effective Approach

- **Reduce Frequency**: Start with **one dedicated deadlift day per week**[[6]](https://www.strongfirst.com/community/threads/deadlift-advice-sought.24801/#post-493404)[[9]](https://www.mensfitness.com/news/68-year-old-powerlifter-workout-routine).
- **Lower the Reps**: Aim for **3 sets of 8-10 reps** with a weight that feels challenging but allows you to maintain a braced, flat back on every single rep[[8]](https://barbend.com/for-strength-gains-avoid-high-rep-training/?utm_source=newsletter.barbend.com&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=3-push-pull-legs-routines-you-ve-gotta-try)[[11]](https://torokhtiy.com/blogs/guides/high-rep-deadlifts).
- **Consider the Trap Bar**: This variation is safer for the lower back and easier to learn, making it ideal for high-rep work[[5]](https://archive.t-nation.com/training/high-rep-deadlifts-are-dead/#content).
- **Listen to Your Body**: If you feel joint pain (not just muscle soreness) or lingering fatigue, dial back the volume or weight further[[3]](https://www.tetongravity.com/forums/printthread.php?t=328980&s=ff0e214bc20bd783135c0e5a773b9e53&pp=40&page=38).

Since you're set on the **5x15** rep scheme, I'll give you the best bang-for-buck exercises that are **joint-friendly** and safe for a 55-year-old body.

Drop the deadlifts for these, and you'll get a great pump, better muscle endurance, and far lower injury risk:

**1. Leg Press (Quad & Glute Focus)**

- **Why:** Zero spinal compression, and you can safely push hard.
- **How:** Place feet shoulder-width and mid-to-low on the platform. Control the descent, but don't lock your knees at the top.

**2. Seated Cable Rows (Upper Back)**

- **Why:** Counteracts "hunching" posture, strengthens the rear delts, and is easy on the lower back.
- **How:** Use the V-grip. Keep your chest tall, squeeze your shoulder blades together, and let your shoulders roll forward on the stretch.

**3. Dumbbell Incline Press (Chest & Shoulders)**

- **Why:** Safer than flat barbell bench for the shoulder joint. The incline reduces rotator cuff impingement.
- **How:** Set the bench to 30-45°. Keep elbows at roughly 45° to your torso (not flared out).

**4. Lat Pulldowns (Back)**

- **Why:** Builds pulling strength without the grip and spinal fatigue of deadlifts.
- **How:** Use a wide or neutral grip. Lean back slightly, pull to your upper chest, and control the release.

**5. Machine Lateral Raises (Side Shoulders)**

- **Why:** Machine versions or cable versions are far kinder to aging rotator cuffs than swinging heavy dumbbells.
- **How:** Focus on tempo—2 seconds up, 3 seconds down. Light weight, high reps shine here.

---

**The Golden Rule for Your 5x15:**\
For **exercises 1-4**, pick a weight where **Reps 13-15 feel like an 8-out-of-10 effort** (you could squeeze 2 more reps if forced). For **#5 (lateral raises)**, drop the weight even lighter—your rotator cuffs will thank you.

Here’s your step-by-step checklist:

---

**Setup:**

- **Stance:** Feet hip-width apart, toes straight or slightly out. Bar over your mid-foot (not over toes).
- **Grip:** Shoulder-width, overhand or hook grip. *Do not mixed grip*—that torques your spine.

---

**The Descent (The Critical Part):**

1. **Unlock knees slightly** (about a 15° bend—*don’t squat down*).
2. **Push your hips BACK** like you’re closing a car door with your glutes. This is the #1 cue.
3. **Keep the bar in contact** with your shins and thighs the entire way down. Drag it along your legs.
4. **Lower until** you feel a deep stretch in your hamstrings—for most, that’s just below the kneecap. **Do NOT go to the floor** like a conventional deadlift. Stop when your back *wants* to round.

---

**The Ascent:**

- **Drive your hips FORWARD**—don’t pull with your lower back. Think: “Hips through the bar.”
- **Keep your chest up** and shoulder blades pinned back. Your neck stays neutral (look 6 feet ahead, not up or down).
- **Squeeze your glutes hard** at the top, but *don't over-arch* your lower back.

---

**The 5x15 Golden Rules for You:**

- **Weight:** Start with just the bar (20kg) or even dumbbells (light). For 15 reps, the weight should feel like a **7/10 effort** on rep 12. If your lower back tires before your hamstrings, **go lighter**.
- **Tempo:** 2 seconds down, 1-second pause at the stretch, 1 second up. Slower = safer + more muscle.
- **Breathe:** Inhale on the way down, exhale forcefully on the way up.
- **Stop immediately** if you feel sharp pain or your lower back rounds—film yourself from the side to check.

---

**Pro-tip for your posture fix:** At the top of every rep, actively **pull your shoulders back and down** as you squeeze your glutes. This trains your brain to stand tall, not hunched.

Since you’ve never done a Face Pull before, you’re in for a treat—this is **the single best exercise for posture, shoulder health, and that "F1 Pitt" broad-shouldered look**. It directly counters all that forward-hunching from desk work, driving, and luggage heaving.

Here’s everything you need to know, from setup to execution, for a 55yo beginner:

---

**What You Need:**

- **Cable machine** with a **rope attachment** (set at about chin height or slightly above).
- If no cable: **Resistance band** anchored at chest height to a doorframe or post.

---

**Step-by-Step Setup:**

1. **Grip the rope** with palms facing each other (neutral grip). Thumbs up.
2. **Step back** until your arms are fully extended forward and there's light tension on the cable.
3. **Stand tall**—chest out, shoulders down, feet shoulder-width apart in a split stance for stability.

---

**The Movement (The "Pulling a Mask" Cue):**

1. **Pull the rope straight toward your face**—not your chest, not your neck. Target is **eye-level to chin level**.
2. **As you pull, externally rotate your hands**—think *"show your palms to the ceiling"* at the end position. Your elbows should end up **above and slightly wider than your shoulders**, like you're doing a double bicep pose but with hands near your temples.
3. **Squeeze your shoulder blades together** hard for 1 second at the peak.
4. **Control the release**—don't let the weight slam back. Take 3 seconds to return to full extension.

---

**Your 55yo Golden Rules:**

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Use light weight** | Start with 2.5kg or 5kg on the stack. *Seriously.* This is a small muscle group. |
| **Focus on rotation** | The external rotation (palms up) is what fixes posture—not just the pull itself. |
| **Don't use momentum** | If you're swinging, it's too heavy. Lower the weight. |
| **Rep range** | 3 sets of 15-20 reps. High reps = safe for rotator cuffs + muscle endurance. |
| **Frequency** | Do them **every session** (Mon/Wed/Fri). Can't overdo these. |

---

**Common Mistakes to Avoid:**

- ❌ Pulling to your chest (that's a row, not a face pull).
- ❌ Elbows dropping down (keep them high at the finish).
- ❌ Shrugging your shoulders up (keep them *down* and back).
- ❌ Using your lower back to lean back (stand tall, brace core).

---

**If You Don't Have a Cable Machine:**

Get a **resistance band** (light-medium tension). Anchor it at chest height on a sturdy pole or doorframe. Same movement. Bands are actually gentler on joints—great for 55yo shoulders.

---

**How You'll Know It's Working:**

- After 2 weeks, your shoulders will feel *more stable* during pressing.
- After 4 weeks, you'll notice your posture looks broader and more open in photos.
